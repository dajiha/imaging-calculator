2 · Background fluorescence Fbg(t)

If the background times differ from the F(t) times, the background is linearly interpolated onto the F(t) time points.

Where to draw the background ROI: on a dark, cell-free area near your ROI, in the same image and over the same frames. Not on a cell: a fluorescent cell bleaches, and that fading would be carried into your trace. A good background trace is nearly flat. If yours clearly decreases over time, it contains fluorescence, so move the ROI.

Photobleaching trend

Updates as you paste. Check two things: the background should be nearly flat, and F − bg should decline smoothly. The dashed line is the bleach fit, shown after you click Calculate.

3 · Photobleaching correction

4 · F0 & normalization

(after background subtraction; applied to all traces)

Both show the same change, written two ways. Fraction = (F − F0)/F0; % = fraction × 100. Example: F0 = 1000 and F drops to 950 → fraction = −0.05, % = −5. Choose whichever your figures or field normally use.

What the calculation does
1. Background: Fc(t) = F(t) − Fbg(t) 2. Bleach fit on Fc over the fit window linear: B(t) = a + b·t (b = bleaching slope) exponential: B(t) = A·exp(−t/τ) (log-linear fit) 3. Correction subtract: Fcorr(t) = Fc(t) − [B(t) − B(t1)] divide: Fcorr(t) = Fc(t) · B(t1) / B(t) 4. F0 = mean Fcorr over baseline frames (or the value you enter) 5. ΔF/F0(t) = [Fcorr(t) − F0] / F0 (×100 for %)
